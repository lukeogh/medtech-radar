Home, round 2, and the applications board: notes

Three ways for the welcome, the tiles and “Needs you today” to share the screen, the full applications board in the Jobs page's Applied view, and a states board. These are proposals: none is picked. Every tile answers one question, shows its numbers in words beside the marks, and never relies on colour alone; charts are plain SVG and CSS with nothing loaded from the internet. Times are UK time.

The three Home variations

A, Stacked band

Variation A reads top to bottom: a full-width welcome strip, one row of four equal tiles (open roles, week by week, applications, Radar's health), then "Needs you today" with its list and detail pane filling what is left of the screen. The page opens on the shape of the day (where the open roles narrow, whether the flow is picking up, where the applications stand, whether Radar is running) and then gives the rest of the screen to the work. The welcome is a strip, not a hero: from 1024px the date and greeting sit at the left and the as-of line and the status sentence beside them, so it costs about 80px. The band is the same four tiles at every width, so the owner learns one place for each answer, and the list below is round-1 Home C's, unchanged in behaviour. All tiles, the welcome and the list come from home2_tiles; this page only places them and sets the band's spacing.

Trade-offs

  • Good at: a one-glance overview before any work; the four questions always sit in the same order and the same place; the list keeps the whole width under the band, so from 1500px of room the company record sits beside the selected item.
  • Costs: the band takes height from the list on every visit, even on a day when the owner only wants the list; at 1280 the band is held to 380px, so a tile whose figures are taller (the applications tile's "Waiting on you" items) scrolls inside the tile under its title and question, with a fade at its foot as the cue. The link out of every tile stays in view. The welcome strip is laid out by page CSS (a grid over the welcome's own parts), and the health tile's "Times are UK time." is hidden because the welcome already prints it.
  • At 2560 (1440 high): tiles are about 600px wide and take their one-line-a-row forms (funnel bars beside their names, six application columns, five health lights across); about 800px of height is left for the list, the item and what is on file.
  • At 1280 (900 high): the band stays four across at about 300px a tile and 380px high; "Needs you today" keeps about 350px: its header, three or four rows and the item's title, actions and first section.
  • On a phone (390): everything stacks in the same order, the tiles full width with 44px targets, the list full bleed with the selected item opened under its own row; the page scrolls and nothing scrolls sideways. The list is a long way down, under four tiles.

B, Work and rail

Home B gives the wide main column to the work: the welcome, then "Needs you today" (round-1 Home C's list and detail pane) filling the rest of the screen's height. The five tiles (open roles, week by week, applications, Radar's health and the map) sit in a rail at the right, under a small "At a glance" heading, and the rail scrolls on its own, so reading a tile never moves the list. From 1800px wide the rail is two columns of 580px tiles: the funnel, the trend and the health lights on the left, the applications board and the map on the right. From 1024 to 1799 it is one 360px column; under 1024 everything is one column, the work first and the tiles after it in the spec's order. It is the only variation with the map, which needs the rail's height more than a band's width.

Trade-offs

  • Good at: the work is the first and largest thing on the page, and the list and detail keep a steady height at every desktop width; the tiles are always one glance to the side and never push the list down.
  • Costs: the tiles are read as a column rather than side by side, so comparing them takes a scroll at 1280; the welcome shares the main column's width rather than the screen's.
  • At 2560 (1440 high): all five tiles fit with no scroll, the rail's 580px columns let the funnel and trend run one line a row, the lights go five across, the board six across and the map's list sits beside it. The main column is about 1310px, so the list and detail sit side by side and what is on file follows the item rather than sitting beside it.
  • At 1280 (900 high): the list and detail get about 650px of height in an 870px column; the 360px rail shows the funnel and most of the trend, and applications, health and the map are reached by scrolling the rail.
  • On a phone (390): one column with 12px gutters: the welcome, "Needs you today" with the selected item open under its row, then the five tiles at full width with 44px targets; no sideways scroll.

C, Welcome hero and bento

The page opens on a warmer, taller welcome: the date, "Good morning, Alex", the status sentence, and its four counts drawn as large figures that each open where those items live (1 urgent signal, 3 Top prospects, 2 One question away, 2 threads due). Radar's five health lights are folded into the foot of the welcome as one strip, light, word and job name, so the state of the system is read in the same glance as the greeting, and the health tile is not drawn. Below it, a bento row sizes the tiles by importance: applications takes the wide cell because it holds the next actions (what waits on the owner), while the funnel and the trend, which only describe the flow, take a medium cell each. "Needs you today", round-1 Home C's list and detail, comes last, so the order on the screen is welcome, then shape, then work. Every part is drawn by the shared home2_tiles functions; this page only places them.

Trade-offs

  • Good at: a calm, readable first screen; the four counts are the biggest targets on the page and lead straight to the work; health is always in view without taking a tile; the applications tile gets room for six columns side by side and its Waiting on you lines.
  • Costs: the work comes third. The welcome and the tiles together are about 730px high at 1280, so the list cannot share a 900px frame with them; health details ("ran 09:02") move off Home to Radar's health; the welcome repeats the list's counts (by design, as links).
  • At 2560 (1440 frame): everything fits without scrolling: the hero (about 310px), the bento in one row (applications about 1250px wide, the funnel and the trend about 620px each) and the list block in the rest (about 600px), with the company's record beside the selected item.
  • At 1280 (900 frame): the content scrolls as one page. The frame shows the hero, the bento and the list block's head; scrolling snaps the block to the top, where it is a full screen high (about 820px) and its list and detail scroll inside it, as on Jobs (see shots/home-C-1280-work-*.png). The bento stays one row, applications twice the width of the others.
  • On a phone (390): one column, applications first, then the funnel and the trend, then the list with the selected item opened under its row. The figures sit two by two and the health strip wraps; targets are 44px and nothing scrolls sideways. It is a long page: the app is about 3,950px and the list starts about 2,240px down.

The applications board

The board is the Jobs page's Applied view in a Board layout. Round-2 Jobs keeps its header and its "Show: Applied" facet, and a "List | Board" switch is added beside them, with Board current. There are six columns: Preparing, Applied, In conversation, Interviewing, Offer and Closed. Radar fills the first two by itself, from things the owner already does elsewhere: saving a tailored CV, and marking a role applied. Both heads say "Filled by Radar". Every later move is the owner's. It takes one click on the item's Move to button, whose menu lists the later columns. Closed appears once for each reason, so no second question follows. The keys do the same, as the menu shows them: M opens the menu, 2 to 5 picks a later column, N, T or W closes with that reason (Closed has no number, because a close always says why), and Z undoes.

An item reads like a Jobs row, not a boxed card. Its title is the link to the role. Under it come the date applied and the company, the CV file that was sent, the days in its column and the next step. "Waiting on you" above the columns gathers the three dated steps, the same three that Home's tile lists. An Applied item with no movement for 21 days closes itself as "no reply". It says so in words and offers "Move back to Applied". From 14 days on, it also says beforehand when the rule will close it. On a phone, a switcher of six counted links picks the one column shown, so nothing scrolls sideways.

Trade-offs

  • Good at: a glance at where every application stands, the next three things to do, and moves that need no drag and no second step. Home and the board read from one module (apps_content.py), so they agree item for item.
  • Costs: six columns need about 2,000px. From 1024 to 1799px the board scrolls sideways inside its own box, with each column 320px wide. This layout was not drawn; only 2560 and the phone were asked for. Three Closed items in the Move to menu make it longer than a menu of columns alone.
  • At 2560: all six columns sit side by side, each scrolling in its own pane. Most of the screen's height is spare at today's 16 items.
  • On a phone: one column at a time. The item with its menu open covers the items under it, as any menu does. Undo sits at the foot of the page.

Decisions taken

  • Divided rows, not cards. Items are divided rows, following design-system §7: no boxes, no shadows on resting items, and 1px rules between columns. The Move to control is the 32px icon button that §7 names ("Move to", key M), not a worded button.
  • Column and reason names. These follow the spec (Preparing, In conversation, Interviewing, Offer, Closed; no reply, turned down, withdrawn), not §7's older "Heard back, Interview, Ended" and "Withdrew, Took the role".
  • Reasons have shapes. No reply has a clock, turned down an x, and withdrawn a turn-back arrow, each beside its word.
  • The 21-day rule covers Applied only. An item in conversation or interviewing has already heard back, so "no reply" would not be true of it.
  • Preparing items close only as withdrawn. They were never sent.
  • Closed items have no Move to. Undo, and Undo last, cover a mistake. Radar's own closed item keeps its single "Move back to Applied".
  • The Show facet counts 16. That is everything on the board, Preparing included. Today's Jobs header says "Applied 7" (sample-data.md). The board's Applied view now holds the applied roles at every stage: 14 sent and 2 being prepared.
  • The roles are partly invented. Nine come from sample-data.md and seven are invented in the same voice. The older applications sent the master CV of the time (cv-20260814-180200.md). The newer ones sent a saved tailored copy, named in the product's style for the fictional identity.
  • The open menu and the Undo notice. The menu is drawn open on "Software Quality Lead, respiratory devices", an item with no next step, so the open menu hides nothing that is due. The Undo notice speaks for "IEC 62304 Consultant", moved to In conversation at 09:12.

Open questions

  1. The funnel's reading. Each step counts its own tier, as the Jobs tier tabs do (530 open, 186 Reading, 45 One question away, 8 Top prospects; 291 filtered out). A true funnel would count "this tier or better" (530, 239, 53, 8). Which should Home show? Each step opens Jobs at that tier either way.
  2. The welcome's name. Every frame says "Good morning, Alex", the fictional identity used across the mockups. The live page would use your first name, from the CV page's settings.
  3. What "Waiting on you" covers. Drawn: steps with a date (follow up, reply, interview prep). Should a Preparing item that has sat unsent for some days join it?
  4. Application steps in "Needs you today". A step due today or overdue (the Wrenfield follow-up) now joins the list as an Applications group and the welcome counts it, so Home gives one answer to what needs you today; steps due later stay on the tile. Your example sentence named four kinds of thing; this makes it five on the typical day. Keep, or leave applications to the tile?
  5. The 21-day rule. Drawn: Radar closes an Applied item as no reply after 21 days without movement, says so on the item and offers Move back to Applied; from day 14 it says when. Should it ask first instead, should 21 be a setting on the CV page, and should In conversation have a longer rule?
  6. The map (variation B only). It answers "where are the open Top and One question away roles?" with a hand-drawn tile map of UK regions and European countries, counts in words and the same figures as a list. Does that change what you do, or does the Jobs location filter already answer it? If it does not earn its place, B's rail has room for one more tile or a taller list.
  7. Which Jobs header count for Applied? Everything on the board (16), what was sent (14) or what is still open (8).
  8. Dragging. Every move works by button and keyboard; dragging is not drawn. Is it wanted at all?

Proposals and what Radar stores today

  • Stored today: the date a role was marked applied; the tier counts; signals with their relevance and week; each job's last run.
  • Proposals drawn here: application stages (Preparing to Closed) and who closed an item and why; the 21-day rule; next steps with due dates and Waiting on you; which CV was sent with each application; weekly Top prospect history for the trend (Radar keeps none today); per-job health words and lateness limits; Done on Home items; the greeting by time of day.
  • Every company, person, role, file and product in these frames is invented.
