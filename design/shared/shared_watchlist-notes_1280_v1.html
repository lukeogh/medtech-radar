Company watchlist, three variations: notes

Caution: Unreviewed. These screens were drawn and self-checked by their authors; the review and fix round was skipped this time, so expect small faults.

A, B and C are labels, not a ranking. Each variation takes one position on the questions the audit left open, and its section says which. Their proposal lists differ in length only because the screens draw different things. Every company, person and product on the screens is invented.

A. Table and record

Rationale

Watchlist A is a table of the companies you chose, one row each, with the latest signal, its relevance, the date found, the thread, the last contact and the state side by side, urgent rows first by a rule written above the table. Selecting a row opens the company’s record beside the table at 2560 and as its own page below 1800, so the reason to act, “Why now”, and the place to act, “Log a contact”, sit together. It reuses the list-beside-detail pattern of Jobs with J and K, and Radar’s part is to suggest companies with a signal at 70 or more, never to add one.

The position it takes

You choose the targets: a company joins the list only when you press Add to watchlist on a signal or on a company’s page. Radar suggests the companies with a signal at 70 or more that are not on the list, and adds nothing to it by itself.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • Every watched company is on one screen, comparable column by column, with the order rule written out and every count matching the rows.
  • At 2560 the record sits beside the table, so reading why it matters and logging a contact happen without leaving the list.
  • It follows the Jobs pattern of a list beside a detail, with J and K, so there is little new to learn.
  • The list stays small and deliberate, and Radar’s suggestion shows without changing it.

Costs

  • Cells are one line, so long headlines and next actions are cut short, at 2560 as well, with the full text in the record.
  • At 1280 and on a phone the record is a separate page, so moving between companies is a page load.
  • A table answers which company before what to do, so the next move is one click away and not in the row.
  • A company with an urgent signal stays off the list until you add it, and with no “not a target” the suggestion can linger.

Proposals it draws

  • Radar’s suggestion line (“1 company has a signal at 70 or more and is not on the list: …”) with “Add Vantage Bio to the watchlist”, and “Added by you on 14 Oct” on a record.
  • A table of the companies on the list: Company, Latest signal, Relevance, Found, Thread, Last contact and State, with the order written above it and “Signals as of the signal watcher’s run at 13 Nov 06:00”.
  • “Search companies” over every company on file, the chips “Urgent 1”, “Thread due 2” and “Window open 3”, a Region facet and sortable column heads.
  • The company record in a pane beside the table at 2560, with Previous and Next (K and J), “Set state”, which gathers today’s three state buttons, and “Remove from watchlist”.
  • “Why now”: the latest signal’s why it matters and next move, with what its relevance rests on (“fixed for every buying window, not scored” or “scored from this one item”).
  • “More actions” on each contact, holding Edit and Delete.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: adding a company from a signal or from its own page, since only the suggestion line’s Add button is drawn.

B. By next move

Rationale

Watchlist B is a to-do list of threads, so its question is what to do next and for which company. The rule puts a company on the list and you only take companies off, and the list is grouped by what you have to do, Act now, Due, Waiting and Watching, each row leading with the next move in words and with when it is due at the right. The company record is a page of its own that opens on the thread with Log a contact drawn open, then the contact log, the signals and the roles on Jobs.

The position it takes

The watchlist is a to-do list of threads, and a company is on it by rule: a signal at 70 or more in the last 90 days, or any contact logged. Nothing is added by hand, and a company you remove stays off until a contact is logged with it.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • Every row answers what to do next in its first line, with the due state beside it, so the page reads as a to-do list.
  • The rule fills the list with no upkeep, so a company with a signal at 70 or more cannot be missed.
  • Overdue and due-today threads have a group of their own, marked with a mark and a word.
  • The record opens on the thread with the form, so acting on a company and logging it happen in one place.

Costs

  • The company is the second thing read on each row, so finding one company is slower than in a table.
  • A company with no signal at 70 or more and no contact cannot be on the list, which leaves off 15 of the typical day’s 24 hand-picked companies.
  • The record is a full page even at 2560, so the list and a record are never read side by side.
  • The list changes by itself as signals pass 90 days, and a company contacted once stays in Waiting until removed.

Proposals it draws

  • The list made by the rule, with the rule written above it (“On the list by rule: a signal at 70 or more in the last 90 days, or any contact logged”), and “Remove from the list”, which keeps a company off until a contact is logged.
  • Groups by next move with counts, Act now, Due, Waiting and Watching, and the header line “10 on the list · 1 to act on now · 2 due, 1 overdue · 5 waiting · 2 watching”.
  • “Leaves the list 10 Feb” on Watching rows: the rule’s 90 days, counted from the newest signal at 70 or more.
  • “Change the next action” to set, move or clear a thread’s next action, and the next action carried over into the form so that a thread is not ended by accident.
  • “Copy the comment” and “Copy the connection note” on a row whose next move needs them, and “Dismiss the signal” on the record.
  • The record as a page of its own, opening on the thread with Log a contact drawn open, and “On the list” saying why the rule holds the company and since when.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: Log a contact opening in place under a row, and the undo after “Save contact” and “Remove from the list”.

C. Timeline

Rationale

Watchlist C lays every signal and contact on one time axis, newest first, with why each matters and the next move in the entry. The timeline starts at today with the next actions due or overdue and then the form that writes the log, and a rail lists the companies by latest activity, each with its state, its thread and who added it. From 1800px the selected company’s record sits at the right, and narrower it is a page of its own built from the same parts.

The position it takes

Timing is the point, so time is the axis and the watchlist reads as a log, newest first, from today. Both you and the rule can add a company, you by name and the rule on a signal at 70 or more in 90 days or a contact logged, and each company says how it came onto the list.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • What happened, and in what order, reads at a glance, with each signal’s why and next move in the entry.
  • The log is written where it is read, and a logged contact appears at the head of the timeline.
  • The company record has the same shape as the list, so moving between them costs nothing to learn.
  • The rail shows each company’s state, its thread or who added it, and its latest activity on one line.

Costs

  • An urgent signal sits at its date and can fall below newer, weaker entries, and at 1280 the open 11 Nov buying window sits at the fold.
  • A heavy week makes a long feed, 66 signals and 22 contacts after a week away, with older entries behind a link.
  • Future due dates are not on the axis, since only due and overdue actions open the timeline.
  • On a phone the companies, their search and “Add a company” are a second tab, one tap away.

Proposals it draws

  • A timeline of signals and contacts across the list, newest first, with All, Signals and Contacts views, which also makes a page of every contact.
  • A rail of companies by latest activity, with “Search companies”, “State: All 24” and “Add a company”.
  • Both ways onto the list, said on each company: “15 added by you, 9 by the rule”, “added by you on 10 Nov”, “On the list by rule: …”.
  • What removal does, said beside “Remove from the watchlist”: the rule adds a company back only on a new signal at 70 or more or a new contact.
  • “Today” at the head of the timeline, the next actions due or overdue, each with “Log a contact for …”, then the Log a contact form with “Prompted by”.
  • Your last visit drawn as a line on the axis, “Your last visit, 12 Nov 18:40” (no visit is recorded today).
  • The record as a pane at the right from 1800px, with “Open as its own page” and its own timeline of contacts and signals.
  • “Show the 33 earlier entries” and “Show the other 6 next actions due”, each with its own address.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: what follows “Add a company”, and a first contact logged with a company that is not on the list.

What all three draw

Proposals that A, B and C share, so the lists under A, B and C leave them out. Each is something the product does not do today.

  • The shared app bar of Jobs round 2, and a Watchlist page in the navigation that lists companies (no such page exists today).
  • Taking a company off the list.
  • A company record laid out from one set of parts, facts, thread, contact log, signals and roles on Jobs, which starts with “Back to Watchlist” when it is a page of its own.
  • Log a contact on screen: date, channel, note, outcome (None, Reply or Conversation), next action and due date (no screen can add a contact today).
  • A contact tied to the signal that prompted it, marking that signal done: a box in A and B, “Prompted by” in C (a contact has no such link today).
  • Threads with a due state, “Due today” and “Overdue by 1 day”, as a mark and a word (nothing marks an overdue thread today).
  • Open urgent signals set apart: the Urgent tag in A and C, the Act now group in B.
  • Each signal’s next move shown with it, in the record in A and on the list and the record in B and C (the company page does not show it today).
  • Signal types, and on the heavy day one row for one event reported by several sources.
  • A buying window’s 90 labelled as fixed for every window and not scored, and the day its window opened.
  • A link from a signal raised from a job advert to its role on Jobs, and the company’s roles drawn as Jobs rows with their CV match (today’s company page prints “Score unscored”).
  • The company’s state shown in the list (today it shows on its own page only).
  • Watcher health on the watchlist: on the stale day a notice, the age on signal figures and “Check now” with what it does beside it (A and C also say when the watcher last ran on a typical day).
  • Addresses for rows and views, so a link from Home can land on a company or its thread.
  • “Times are UK time”, said once.

Where the working view and the product differ

From the audit’s design facts, as the product stood on 13 Nov 2026.

  • There is no watchlist: no page lists companies, the navigation has no entry for one, and nothing marks a company as a target. In the code the word “watchlist” means the list of news sources.
  • Company records make themselves from every advert and signal, at least 2,431 on the day and 801 linked from a page, so recruiters, large employers, institutes and the news sources themselves all have company pages.
  • A company page is reached from a company name on Insights, from inside an opened role on Jobs, or by typing its number; there is no index and no search.
  • “Buying window” in the code means any new advert from a company already in the contact log, always set to 90, and none has been raised. The working view’s meaning, a first hire in quality, regulatory or software leadership, is the first-hire tripwire, which since 5 Nov scores under 40, and software-leadership hires are not detected at all.
  • The contact log is empty, and no screen can add, edit or delete a contact: contacts are written from a command line, by “I did it” on Insights, or by a change of state on a company page.
  • A thread is not stored: it is the newest contact that carries a next action, none exists, and nothing marks one overdue.
  • Why a signal matters is a line with no label, and its next move is not shown on the company page.
  • Nothing counts or groups the signals at 75 or more, and none has reached the phone.
  • Companies first met through a signal are never enriched, so their facts read “not known” and their region “Elsewhere”.
  • A company’s state shows on its own page only: no list, count or rule reads it, and every company reads “seen” today.

Questions that block the choice

These decide between the variations, so they come first.

  • Who puts a company on the list: you alone, with Radar suggesting (A), the rule alone (B), or both, with each company saying which (C)?
  • What should the page answer first: which company (A’s table), what to do next (B’s groups), or what happened when (C’s timeline)?
  • Should the company record sit beside the list on a wide screen (A and C), or always be a page of its own (B)?

Also open, whichever is chosen

  • What a buying window is: the code’s meaning, any new advert from a company already contacted, fixed at 90, or the working view’s, a first hire in quality, regulatory or software leadership.
  • If Insights C is chosen, its By company view is the watchlist: should it then take the layout chosen here, or stay a view of the signals table?
