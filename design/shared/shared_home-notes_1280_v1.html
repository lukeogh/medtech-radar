Home, three variations: notes

Caution: Unreviewed. These screens were drawn and self-checked by their authors; the review and fix round was skipped this time, so expect small faults.

A, B and C are labels, not a ranking. Each variation takes one position on the questions the audit left open, and its section says which. Their proposal lists differ in length only because the screens draw different things. Every company, person and product on the screens is invented.

A. One list

Rationale

Home A answers the ten-second question with one list, “Needs you today”, most urgent first, in which each row is one count, one fact and one link that lands on the item or on the filtered list. Radar faults lead, because a job that has not reported makes the figures under it doubtful, and urgent signals, new roles and threads due follow. Health is a one-line strip under the page header with a table behind it, in the right third at 2560, and what is open but neither new nor urgent is counted in one line under the list.

The position it takes

“Needs you today” means new since your last visit, plus anything urgent that is still not dealt with: Radar faults, open signals at 75 or more, and threads due today or overdue. Signals under 75 and roles seen before the last visit stay off the list and are counted in one line under it.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • One place to look and one order to read in, with every row ending in a link that names the item and the page it lands on.
  • Each relevance figure sits beside its reason and its source, and a buying window’s 90 says that it is set by rule.
  • Health reads in one line at every width, with each job’s last run, next run, state and mode a glance away in the Radar table.
  • Home only reads: no button on it writes, spends money or sends anything to the phone.

Costs

  • It needs a record of each visit, which the product does not keep, and someone has to decide when a visit ends.
  • Signals from 40 to 74, the digest’s bar of 70 included, never reach the list however new they are.
  • Nothing can be dealt with on Home: every action, Check now included, is a click away on another page.
  • Rows that carry a reason and a source are tall, so on the heavy day the 16 rows scroll inside their pane at 1280.

Proposals it draws

  • “New” measured from your last visit, with “Last visit 12 Nov 18:40, 15 hours ago” in the page header (no visit is recorded today).
  • A health strip under the page header: the verdict, then one phrase for each of the five jobs.
  • A Radar table giving each job’s schedule, last run, next run, state, mode and what it reported, with a fault row on the list linking to its job there (“See the signal watcher in the Radar table”).
  • “Nothing else is waiting”: what is at nought, as plain sentences, then a counted line of what is open but left off the list.
  • “Since Monday”: this week against last week as sentences, in place of today’s charts.
  • The bar of 40 used on Home, in “16 signals from 40 to 74, under the urgent bar” (no page uses it today).
  • A buying window’s figure labelled “Relevance 90, set by rule” (the 90 is set in code today and shown as if scored).
  • The health check’s own answer read on Home, with what it covers: the inbox and the signal watcher only.
  • Links that land on the new roles of one tier on Jobs and on one company’s thread on the watchlist.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: nothing, since every proposal in its report appears on at least one frame.

B. Three panes by destination

Rationale

Home B sorts what needs you by where it gets dealt with, in three panes: Jobs, Signals and Radar. Each pane opens with its own answer and lists at most five items, each a link to that item, with every count taken over two fixed windows, today and since Monday. Radar is a table of the five jobs at 2560, a strip above the two lists at 1280 and a list on a phone, and the threads due are one line under the Signals answer.

The position it takes

“Needs you today” means what arrived in two fixed windows, today and since Monday, and nothing records a visit. An urgent signal counts while it is still open and was found since Monday, so on the next Monday it drops off Home.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • Each pane answers for its own destination in its first line, so you can stop after the one pane you need.
  • The page load writes nothing and nothing is stored, so the same hour reads the same on every device.
  • The windows reset each Monday and each pane lists five items at most, so Home stays short however long you were away.
  • At 2560 the Radar table shows each job’s last run, next run, state, mode and errors in one view.

Costs

  • There are three answers to read in place of one, and nothing ranks a Radar fault against a new role.
  • There is no “new since last time”, so a role you saw last night stays under “Earlier this week” until Monday.
  • After a week away only what arrived since Monday is counted, 10 of the 14 new roles on the heavy day, and an urgent signal from before Monday leaves Home while still open.
  • Threads due are one line with a count and a link, with no company named on Home.

Proposals it draws

  • Two fixed windows, today and since Monday, with a first-line answer in each pane, such as “3 new Top prospects since Monday, 2 of them today”.
  • Up to five roles and five signals named on Home, each landing on that role on Jobs or that signal on Insights, with “Show all 10 since Monday, on Jobs” and “Show all 9 at 40 or more since Monday, on Insights”.
  • The signal type on each signal row, and the “Urgent” tag, a mark and a word, on an open signal at 75 or more.
  • “Check now” on Home on every day, with a line beside it saying that it can take up to ten minutes and may notify the phone (today it is on the Archive only).
  • A Radar pane: the five jobs as a table at 2560, a strip of five cells at 1280 and a list on a phone, with “What was checked” giving what each job read or sent.
  • The health check’s own answer read on Home, with what it covers: the inbox and the signal watcher only.
  • “2 threads due: 1 today, 1 overdue” under the Signals answer, with “Open the threads due, on the Watchlist”.
  • “Re-score them on the CV page” beside roles that could not be scored, and “See the 26 sources on Insights” beside sources that could not be read.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: the Radar pane moving to the top on a phone when a job needs a look, since the phone frame shows the typical day.

C. Status sentence and inbox

Rationale

Home C treats Home as a triage inbox whose one heading gives the whole answer, with one item in the list under it for each thing the heading counts. Each item opens where it lives or is marked Done from the keyboard, moving with J and K as on Jobs, and its detail sits beside the list, so most items can be judged without leaving Home. Radar’s health is a quiet line at the foot of the page while all is well, and a job that needs a look becomes the first item.

The position it takes

Home is a triage inbox with memory: an item stays until you mark it Done, and Done is stored, so the list is the same on every visit and every device. “Needs you today” means everything that has arrived and not been marked Done, plus every Radar job that needs a look.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • One sentence answers the ten-second question, and each count in it is the size of a group in the list under it.
  • Each item opens where it lives, with J, K and Enter as on Jobs and E added for Done, so little is new to learn.
  • The pane beside the list lets most items be judged without leaving Home, with the company’s record beside it at 2560.
  • Done is remembered, so nothing already dealt with comes back on the next visit or on the phone.

Costs

  • Done, Undo last and the Done view are a new store and a new write, kept in step with three other pages.
  • Done on Home changes nothing where the item lives, so it is a third “dealt with” mark beside the ones on Insights and Jobs.
  • An inbox asks to be emptied: after a week away it holds 28 items, and it grows while nothing is marked Done.
  • The pane repeats parts of Jobs, Insights and the watchlist, so the same records are drawn on four pages and must agree.

Proposals it draws

  • The status sentence as the page’s one heading, from “Four things need you: …” to “Nothing needs you today”.
  • An inbox of items in five groups (Radar, Urgent signals, Top prospects, One question away, Threads due), each item with its own address, such as /?item=s-hartwell-window.
  • Done (key E), a “To do | Done” view and Undo last: a stored record of what has been dealt with on Home.
  • J and K to move, Enter to open and E for Done, with Previous and Next.
  • The selected item’s detail beside the list: a role’s score and details, a signal’s why it matters, relevance with what it rests on, next move, type and publication date, or a job’s fault, last runs and schedule.
  • “On file for Hartwell Infusion Ltd” at 2560: the company’s state (“Window open since 11 Nov”), its thread, its signals on Insights and its roles on Jobs, with “Everything on file for Hartwell Infusion Ltd”.
  • A link from a signal raised from a job advert to that role on Jobs, under “Read from”.
  • “Relevance 90” with “Set by rule, not judged: every buying window is 90”.
  • “Check now” beside a stalled signal watcher, with what it does to the phone (today it is on the Archive only).

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: the Done view, a write in flight, a refusal message and a thread’s link on to the watchlist (“Open the thread on Watchlist”).

What all three draw

Proposals that A, B and C share, so the lists under A, B and C leave them out. Each is something the product does not do today.

  • The shared app bar of Jobs round 2: Watchlist and CV in the navigation (today it holds Home, Jobs and Insights), the command box and the inbox status line.
  • A statement of what needs you today, naming the roles and signals behind it (A and C name the threads too), with each link landing on the item or on a filtered list (today Home names none of them, and nothing inside a page has an address).
  • A count of open signals at 75 or more, set apart from the rest (no page counts them today).
  • Threads due today or overdue, with an overdue mark (no thread exists today, and nothing marks an overdue one).
  • One verdict on health, such as “All five jobs ran on time” or “3 of 5 jobs need a look”.
  • The advert checker, the Monday digest and the phone push reported on Home, each with its mode: armed, in dry run or off (today Home shows none of the three).
  • A next run for each job, and a lateness limit, drawn for the signal watcher as 7 hours on a 6-hour schedule, where the product runs it every two hours.
  • Errors named: sources that could not be read, roles that could not be scored and scheduled runs that left no record (today Home shows failed emails only).
  • Roles waiting to be scored as a count (there is no queue today).
  • The age of every figure from a stale job, such as “Signals as of 12 Nov 11:14, 22 hours old”.
  • “Times are UK time”, said once (every time is UTC and unlabelled today).
  • Settings off Home: the scorer fields and “Add a job source” move to the CV page, behind one quiet link.
  • A link to the workflow scheduler from Home (today it is in the Archive footer only).
  • On the heavy day, one row for one event reported by several sources, “Reported by 3 sources” (today each report is its own signal).
  • A typical day on which the phone push is armed and has pushed, and the Monday digest is armed and has sent (nothing has been pushed so far, and the digest has only run in dry run).
  • Roles counted as Jobs counts them, not adverts (Home counts 1,211 adverts today where Jobs shows 910 roles).

Where the working view and the product differ

From the audit’s design facts, as the product stood on 13 Nov 2026.

  • The working view has Home say whether anything needs you today and take you straight to it. Home today shows totals, three link cards, six widgets of trends and plumbing, the scorer’s settings, a “Needs you” box of up to three backlog counts, a contact tally, a form to add a job board and a footer of machinery facts, and it names no role, signal, company or thread.
  • There is no “today” to measure from: nothing records a visit or what you have seen, and a page load cannot write, so “new since last time” needs a deliberate write or a note kept in the browser.
  • A link can land only on the top of a page, because no section, no role on Jobs and no signal has an address.
  • News is not a separate thing: Radar collects one kind of item, the company signal, and no regulator or standards body is among its sources.
  • Health as the working view describes it is only partly there. Home reports the inbox, with a light and its time, and gives the signal watcher one footer line with no light or age; the advert checker’s runs are recorded and shown nowhere, and nothing says whether the digest or the phone push is armed.
  • On the audit’s day the signal watcher had not finished a run for 22.6 hours, which the health reply calls degraded, while Home showed only “Signals last checked 13 Nov 00:00” in its footer. Nothing had ever been pushed to the phone, and the weekly digest had only been built in dry run.
  • The only errors shown are failed emails: failing sources are named on Insights only, and a run that crashes leaves no record at all.
  • There is no queue of roles waiting to be scored: Home shows roles with no tier and a command to run on the server, and on the day it said “7 roles unscored” when none was waiting for a first score.
  • The inbox light turns amber after any quiet two hours and red after six, so a quiet night reads as stalled, and none of the limits in use fits a job that runs once a day.
  • Home counts adverts (1,211 open on the day) where Jobs counts roles (910), and every time on every page is UTC with nothing to say so.

Questions that block the choice

These decide between the variations, so they come first.

  • Should Home keep a memory, and of what: the time of your last visit (A), nothing (B), or each item you mark Done (C)? The same answer sets what a week away looks like: everything since your last visit, only what arrived since Monday, or everything not yet marked Done.
  • Should anything be dealt with on Home itself? A only reads, B offers Check now, and C marks items Done and offers Check now when the signal watcher has stalled.
  • Which signals reach Home: those at 75 or more only (A and C), or also up to five from 40 to 74 (B)?

Also open, whichever is chosen

  • The lateness limit for each job: only the signal watcher’s 7 hours is drawn, and the reports propose 3 hours for the inbox, 26 for the advert checker, and 7 days and 2 hours for the digest; and whether the signal watcher runs every two hours, as the product does, or every six, as drawn.
  • Whether Home counts roles, as all three draw and as Jobs does, or adverts, as Home does today.
