MedTech Radar

CV: notes

Three variations of the CV page, drawn for the owner to choose between: A, Three panes; B, Asks grid; C, Margin notes. The names come from the Prepare application page, because each variation is one answer for both pages; that page's notes are in notes-prepare. Each is drawn on its own terms and stated evenly; none is ranked, recommended or merged.

A proposal is anything a frame draws that the product does not do today, read against the ground truth of main on 13 Nov 2026. The CV, its employers and every name are fictional.

Words used here: a pane is one of the areas that sit side by side on the screen; a band is a strip across its full width; a rail is a column that stays at one side; a drawer is a panel that opens over the page and closes again; a tint is a faint colour behind text; the primary action is the one button drawn filled.

A: Three panes

Rationale

A keeps three things in view at 2560: a slim toolbar, the master CV as two A4 pages side by side at 100% on white paper, and a rail for everything about the master that is not its text. The rail says whether any scores are stale, lists the versions with the one the scorer reads, shows the scoring preferences read only, and holds the one-off import. Page-level actions sit in one fixed toolbar of icons, with Save as a new version as the one primary action, and an item's controls show in the right margin only for the item under the pointer or the focus. Line numbers are a switch, off by default, and sections are reordered in a separate Rearrange mode, in which each section folds to its heading, its size and its opening words, with Move up and Move down. At 1280 the pages stack in one column beside the rail, and on a phone the rail's parts become tabs and the sheet reflows to a reading view in the same type, a white page at a time.

Trade-offs

  • Gives: At 2560 both pages and the whole rail are in view with no scrolling, so the version the scorer reads, the stale-scores state and the preferences sit beside the text being edited.

    Costs: The rail always takes width (about 850px at 2560, 389px at 1280, 320px from 1024 to 1279px), and at 2560 its lower half is empty.

  • Gives: White paper laid out line for line as the Word file, so line lengths and page breaks are the download's.

    Costs: Two white A4 sheets, about 1,610 by 1,120px, are the brightest thing on a dark screen.

  • Gives: The sheet is at 100% from 1280px up, and on a phone it reflows to a reading view in the sheet's own type.

    Costs: From 1024 to 1279px it is scaled to 90% or 75%, and on a phone the page shape gives way to the reading view, so what is seen there is not the printed page.

  • Gives: A toolbar of icons 48px wide holds every page-level action in one fixed place.

    Costs: The actions carry no visible words, so they are learnt from the names shown on hover and read out by a screen reader, and Save as a new version is a filled icon button rather than a labelled one.

  • Gives: Rearrange mode gives an overview of the section order and keeps move controls off every heading in normal editing.

    Costs: Moving a section takes the switch, the moves and Done, and the sections' text is folded away while moving.

  • Gives: Line numbers hidden by default keep the sheet looking like the document.

    Costs: Checking a cited line means turning the switch on first.

  • Gives: A fixed place at the top of the rail where the count and the cost of stale scores appear after a save, with the confirm in the same place.

    Costs: The slot takes a line even when nothing is pending.

  • Gives: The versions are always listed, and View opens an older one read only, numbered as a score made on it cites it.

    Costs: The list grows with every save and needs a cut-off or a Show all once there are many versions.

What it borrows

From the Enhancv model

  • The sheet as the interface: two white A4 pages at real size, typed into where they stand.
  • A separate view for reordering sections, as Enhancv's Rearrange dialog is, with the two changes the study draws from its sessions: each section keeps its opening words, and Move up and Move down replace dragging.
  • A slim toolbar of icons at the left, where Enhancv kept its toolbox from 2024 to early 2026, with Undo and Redo always on screen, and the save state in words beside the document's name.

From other products

  • Two pages side by side, as a word processor's multiple-page view shows a spread.
  • A rail of properties and versions beside the document, as a document editor keeps its version history in a side panel.

B: Asks grid

Rationale

B keeps the CV's structure in an outline beside the sheet: every section and entry with the lines it runs over, split where Word starts a page, and with Move up and Move down always there, so reordering needs no mode. The sheet is one page column at 100% on dimmed paper in the Word file's layout, and shows a line number only for the line being edited, while the outline and Go to line give the rest. Page actions sit in a labelled toolbar (Save version, History, Preferences, Import), and history, scoring preferences and the import open from it as drawers, out of view until wanted. Anything about the CV as a whole, such as roles still scored on an old version, is a notice line under the page header. At 1280 the outline narrows beside the sheet and an open drawer covers it, and on a phone the outline is the first view, with rows that open to their lines and the page as a second view.

Trade-offs

  • Gives: The whole structure and every item's line range are in view beside the sheet at every desktop width, and a section or an entry moves with one press and no mode.

    Costs: The outline repeats the headings beside the sheet, so the structure is drawn twice.

  • Gives: The sheet reads as the download, with no number except on the line being edited.

    Costs: A bullet's number takes a step: put the focus on the line, read the range in the outline, or use Go to line; nothing shows every number at once.

  • Gives: Dimmed paper means less glare on a dark screen, and the Word file's layout is unchanged.

    Costs: Dimmed paper is not literally white, so the download looks brighter than the screen.

  • Gives: History, the preferences and the import stay out of the way until opened, and from 1800px an open drawer covers only the empty end of the outline's rows.

    Costs: History and the preferences are one press away, not always in view, and at 1280 an open drawer covers the outline and dims the rest.

  • Gives: Every page action has its name on screen, with no icon to learn.

    Costs: The labelled toolbar is 72px wide against 48px for icons alone, and “Preferences” breaks with a hyphen to fit.

  • Gives: One page column leaves the outline its room beside the sheet.

    Costs: Page 2 needs a scroll even at 2560.

  • Gives: On a phone the outline is the first view, with 15px text and small edits in place.

    Costs: There the page itself is seen only in the Page view, scaled to about 43%, to see where pages break.

  • Gives: The outline stays beside the sheet down to 1024px.

    Costs: At 1280 the outline is about 366px wide, so long entry headings wrap to three or four lines, and from 1024 to 1279px the sheet is drawn at 75% to leave the outline about 300px.

C: Margin notes

Rationale

C keeps the master CV at its real A4 width in one page column and puts history, preferences and the import behind three tabs in one column beside it, so the sheet is never narrowed whichever tab is open. The sheet is drawn in the app's dark tokens with light ink, and a Paper preview switch shows it white, as the Word file prints. Line numbers are a switch, off by default, and also show by themselves beside the lines that asks cite, which is where a number says a score leans on that line. Sections move with Move up and Move down among their own controls, shown on hover and focus, so reordering needs no mode, and notices that apply only sometimes, such as stale scores, open as a line above the sheet. At 1280 the sheet stays at 100% beside the tabbed column, and on a phone the CV reflows to a reading view with its cited numbers in a narrow column at its left and the tabs under it.

Trade-offs

  • Gives: The sheet keeps its full 794px width at every desktop width from 1280px up, whichever tab is open.

    Costs: History, Preferences and Import are never in view together, so checking a version against the preferences takes a tab switch, and from 1024 to 1279px the sheet is scaled to 78%.

  • Gives: No white page glares on a dark screen, and the app keeps one look.

    Costs: What is on screen is not literally what prints until Paper preview is switched on, and under the Light appearance the sheet stays dark inside a light app.

  • Gives: Sections are reordered where they stand, with buttons that work from the keyboard and no mode to enter or leave.

    Costs: A large reorder takes many presses of Move up or Move down, and there is no overview of all the sections at once.

  • Gives: Notices appear only when they apply, in place above the sheet, and the stale-scores line states the count and the cost before any button.

    Costs: A notice put off with Cancel has no other home on this page.

  • Gives: Only the lines a score leans on show their numbers, so a cited line is marked before it is changed.

    Costs: A bare number does not say which role or which ask cites it; the role's own page does.

  • Gives: Save as a new version is drawn filled only while there are unsaved changes, so the toolbar shows when a save is due.

    Costs: With nothing to save no button stands out, and Save as a new version looks like the tools beside it.

  • Gives: One page column matches how Word sets the pages.

    Costs: At 2560 page 2 needs a scroll, and the tabbed column is wider than any one tab needs, so its lower part is empty.

  • Gives: On a phone the CV reflows into one column with the same lines, numbers and controls.

    Costs: On a phone the page shape is lost, and a page break shows only as a rule.

Where the brief and the build differ

From the ground truth of what is built (its sections 5 and 6): what a designer or the owner has to know before choosing.

  • Line numbers are positions, not identities. Every line of the CV text is numbered from 1, headings and the blank line before each heading included, and a number shifts when a line is added or removed above; a citation survives an edit because the cited line is found again by its words. The brief's stable number on every line is not built; all three variations hide the numbers by default, and when they show, blank lines show none, so the visible numbers skip.
  • An experience entry is one heading line of free text, “Job title, Company (what it makes), 2022 to 2024”, with its lines and bullets under it. The brief's company, job title, dates and location have no fields of their own, and the Word template has no tabs or tables, so nothing can sit at the right-hand edge.
  • The brief's typical CV is two pages of about 120 lines. In the Word template a CV of about 112 numbered lines took three pages, so two pages hold about 90: the sample was cut to 94 numbered lines, which Word sets on two pages with page 2 starting at line 49.
  • The brief's slim vertical toolbar follows the layout Enhancv used from 2024 to early 2026. In spring 2026 Enhancv began moving those tools to a bar across the top (its help note of 7 Apr 2026 says some users see it), and whether the left toolbar is still served could not be established. All three variations keep a toolbar at the left, as the brief asks.
  • The theme defaults to Auto, which follows the operating system, with Light and Dark as choices set on Home or Jobs; the CV page has no Appearance control today. The brief calls the current theme dark: every frame is drawn dark, with the control in the app bar.
  • Today the CV page's navigation lists Home, Jobs and Insights, with no CV link and no page marked as current; the page is reached from Home and from the Prepare page.
  • Only LinkedIn adverts can be fetched, so only a LinkedIn role can hold a score made on its job description, and such a score is never re-scored after a new master, from the CV page or the Prepare page.
  • Jobs says “full job spec” and the Prepare page says “full description”; these designs say “the job description” in sentences and keep the tag “Full job spec”.
  • The CV is not drawn as a page today: it is a column of numbered one-line fields in the system font, with uppercase section headings, on the app's own ground. The Word file is A4 and Arial, in sentence case, so what is on screen does not match what is downloaded.
  • Saving is one button, “Save as a new version”, enabled whether or not anything changed; a save that changes nothing writes nothing, and the only sign of a save is the page reloading with the new version's name. Nothing labels unsaved changes but the browser's leave-page prompt.
  • A save made from a page opened before another tab saved is refused, in red under the button, with the name of the version now in use.
  • There is no undo beyond the browser's own inside a field. Deleting a line asks nothing; deleting an entry or a section asks first.
  • The add buttons, “+ Line”, “+ Bullet”, “+ Entry” and “+ Headline line”, are always on screen. Only the move, bullet and delete controls wait for hover or focus, and a line's show all the time on touch screens and at 600px and under.
  • An empty section shows no words and no number, and is left out of the text and the Word file.
  • History is a list of file names, always on the page; it does not open or close. No version can be viewed, compared or restored from the page; an older one is made active only by editing, by hand, the file that names the version in use.
  • Sections cannot be reordered or added today.
  • Stale scores have no flag and no count. One press of “Re-score stale items” reaches at most 25 roles, and only roles whose score still holds the single combined figure the scorer gave before 3 Aug 2026; a score made since then has no such figure, so a role first scored after that date is never reached. The confirm is the browser's, and its cost reads “This spends real tokens.”
  • The scoring preferences are on Home, where Title, Day-rate floor and Keywords are edited. The location and sector rules are prose in the preferences file, the CV match pass mark of 70 and the Filtered floor of 40 are shown nowhere, and no date is stored for any preference.
  • The one-off import is always on the page and takes md, txt and pdf files as well as Word files.
  • Caps the brief does not mention: a save needs a CV of 20,000 characters, 400 lines and 1,000 characters on a line or fewer; an upload is at most 5 MB; and an extracted text waits 24 hours for its confirm.

Questions

Questions that bear on the choice between A, B and C

  1. How should the paper sit in the dark app: white, as the Word file prints (A), a dimmed grey with dark ink (B), or dark with a Paper preview switch (C)? The same choice holds on the Prepare application page.
  2. How should sections be reordered: in a Rearrange mode that folds each section to its heading, its size and its opening words (A), from the outline at any time (B), or with Move up and Move down among each section's own controls (C)?
  3. How should line numbers show: a switch for every number (A), the line in focus with each item's range in the outline and Go to line (B), or a switch plus the numbers of cited lines by themselves (C)?
  4. Where should the history and the preferences live: always in view in a rail (A), one press away in drawers (B), or in tabs beside the sheet (C)?
  5. On a phone, which first view: a reflowed reading view of the sheet (A, C), or the outline with rows that open (B, which also has a Page view of the sheet at about 43%)? Is reading with small edits enough there, or should the phone also show the page shape?

Questions for one variation, if it is chosen

Three for each variation.

  1. In A, the rail keeps about 850px at 2560 and its lower half is empty. Should the rail be narrower there?
  2. In A, the stale-scores line stays at the top of the rail even when nothing is pending. Should it show only after a new version?
  3. In A, the list of versions grows with every save. How many should the rail show before a Show all?
  4. In B, nothing shows every line number at once. Are the line in focus, the outline's ranges and Go to line enough to check a citation such as “CV line 34”?
  5. In B, at 1280 an open drawer covers the outline and dims the rest. Should a drawer at that width leave the outline in view?
  6. In B, “Preferences” breaks with a hyphen in the 72px toolbar. Should the toolbar be wider, or the label shorter?
  7. In C, after Cancel, should the stale-scores line open again on the next visit, or should the history carry the count?
  8. In C, should a cited line's number say, on hover or focus, which role and which ask cite it?
  9. In C, should the tabbed column open on the tab last used, and Paper preview stay as it was left on both pages, or should each start on History and the dark sheet?

Questions that stand whichever is chosen

  1. Should one confirmed run re-score every stale role (A and C draw 37 in one press), or keep today's 25 a press (B draws runs of up to 25)? Today's run also skips every role first scored since 3 Aug 2026, whose score has no combined figure: should it reach them?
  2. Should View open an older version read only on the sheet, as all three offer and A draws open? Should an older version ever be made the one the scorer reads from this page, and should two versions be comparable?
  3. Should the scoring preferences stay read only here, edited on Home and in the preferences file, or be edited on this page?
  4. “Last changed 11 Nov” needs a date that is not stored today. Should Radar record when the preferences file changes?
  5. Are Undo and Redo wanted (A and C draw them)? Deleting a line asks nothing today, so Undo would be the only way back. Should there also be one action that discards every unsaved edit?
  6. The brief asks for a stable number on every line; the build numbers lines by position, so a number moves when a line is added above. Accept position numbers, or build a stable id for each line?
  7. Should “Save as a new version” stay enabled while nothing has changed, as today (a save that changes nothing writes nothing), or be unavailable until a line is typed? A draws it filled as the primary action whenever it can be used, B always, and C only while there are unsaved changes.
  8. The list of versions is called Versions in A and History in B and C. Which word should the product use?
  9. Should Title and Keywords, the two lines edited on Home, be listed with the read-only preferences? None of the three lists them.
