Prepare application: notes

Three variations of the Prepare application page, drawn for the owner to choose between: A, Three panes; B, Asks grid; C, Margin notes. Each is drawn on its own terms and stated evenly; none is ranked, recommended or merged.

Each variation is one answer for both pages, so the CV page's notes, in notes-cv, follow the same three. A proposal is anything a frame draws that the product does not do today, read against the ground truth of main on 13 Nov 2026. The role, the CV, the advert and every name are fictional.

Words used here: a pane is one of the areas that sit side by side on the screen; a band is a strip across its full width; a rail is a column that stays at one side; a drawer is a panel that opens over the page and closes again; a tint is a faint colour behind text; the primary action is the one button drawn filled.

A: Three panes

Rationale

A puts what decides the application in one band under the page header: both scores with what each was based on, the four gates, the rate line, and the actions in the order they are used. Below the band three panes hold the work: at 2560 the tailored copy on a white sheet at full size, the asks, and the advert as it is stored. Evidence is linked by highlight alone: the selected ask lights its cited lines on the sheet, and the line in focus lights the asks that cite it, so each ask keeps to one row. At 1280 the band keeps the two scores and the actions, the gates and the rate line fold into Score details, and the advert becomes a tab beside the asks. On a phone the asks lead, with the CV and the advert as tabs and Save CV and Mark applied in a bar at the foot.

Trade-offs

  • Gives: At 2560 both scores, the gates, the rate line, every action, all 18 asks and the selected ask's lines are in view with no scrolling.

    Costs: The band takes about 170px of height at 2560 and about 110px at 1280, and at 1024 it wraps to about 200px, so the panes are shorter.

  • Gives: A white sheet that looks as the Word download prints.

    Costs: It is the brightest thing on a dark screen.

  • Gives: Evidence by highlight keeps each ask to one row, so the list stays short.

    Costs: The list shows cited line numbers, not the cited words, so reading a citation means looking across to the sheet.

  • Gives: The actions sit in one row in the order they are used, with Rescore role's cost beside it.

    Costs: The actions sit away from the CV they act on.

  • Gives: The advert is shown as it is stored, with nothing invented about its structure.

    Costs: Its headings read like body text, and even this 3,227-character advert needs a scroll at 2560; a typical one of 5,000 to 10,000 characters always will.

  • Gives: At 1280 the sheet sits beside the asks, at 80%.

    Costs: Its body text is then about 11px, and about 10px from 1024 to 1239px: small for editing in place. The asks and the advert cannot be seen together, and the gates and the rate line are one press away in Score details.

  • Gives: The selected ask stays selected, so its lines stay lit while the copy is edited.

    Costs: Two kinds of highlight can be on at once, the selected ask's lines and the asks citing the line in focus, and the reader has to tell them apart.

  • Gives: On a phone the asks lead, and Save CV and Mark applied stay in a bar at the foot.

    Costs: Rescore role, Download and Open the advert on LinkedIn are one tap deeper, in More, and the asks and the CV are never in view together.

From the Enhancv model

  • A slim toolbar of icons at the canvas's left, where Enhancv kept its toolbox from 2024 to early 2026, with Undo and Redo always on screen.
  • One item's controls at a time, in the margin outside the text, not on the item's top edge, where Enhancv's bar covers the line above.

From other products

  • Highlighting that runs both ways, as a spreadsheet can trace the cells a formula reads and the cells that read it.
  • The work, its findings and its source in panes side by side, as a code editor can set a file beside its list of problems and a preview.

C: Margin notes

Rationale

C places each ask beside the CV line that evidences it, as a reviewer's comments sit beside a manuscript, so where the CV answers an ask is read by position. The tailored copy stays at the centre at full size on a dark sheet in the app's own tokens, with a Paper preview switch that shows it white, as the Word file prints. Where the brief and the build differ

From the ground truth of what is built (its sections 5 and 6): what a designer or the owner has to know before choosing.

  • Line numbers are positions, not identities. Every line of the CV text is numbered from 1, headings and the blank line before each heading included, and a number shifts when a line is added or removed above. A citation survives an edit because the cited line is found again by its words: a moved line shows “(14 when scored)”, and a cited line whose words changed makes its ask stale. The brief's stable number on every line is not built; all three variations hide the numbers by default, and when they show, blank lines show none, so the visible numbers skip.
  • The copy is not drawn as a page today. The same editor as the CV page draws it as a column of numbered one-line fields in the system font, so what is on screen does not match the Word file that is downloaded.
  • An experience entry is one heading line of free text, “Job title, Company (what it makes), 2022 to 2024”, with its lines and bullets under it. There are no separate fields for company, job title, dates or location, and the Word template has no tabs or tables, so nothing can sit at the right-hand edge.
  • The brief's typical CV is two pages of about 120 lines. In the Word template a CV of about 112 numbered lines took three pages, so two pages hold about 90: the sample was cut to 94 numbered lines, which Word sets on two pages with page 2 starting at line 49.
  • The brief's slim vertical toolbar follows the layout Enhancv used from 2024 to early 2026. In spring 2026 Enhancv began moving those tools to a bar across the top (its help note of 7 Apr 2026 says some users see it), and whether the left toolbar is still served could not be established. All three variations keep a toolbar at the left, as the brief asks.
  • The theme defaults to Auto, which follows the operating system, with Light and Dark as choices set on Home or Jobs; these two pages have no Appearance control today. The brief calls the current theme dark: every frame is drawn dark, with the control in the app bar.
  • Today this page's navigation lists Home, Jobs, Insights and CV and marks no page as current; the CV page's lists Home, Jobs and Insights.
  • Jobs says “full job spec” (its tag, and “Scored on the full job spec” in a row's detail) and the Prepare page says “full description”. These designs say “the job description” in sentences and keep the tag “Full job spec”.
  • Only LinkedIn adverts can be fetched. A role from Reed, Indeed or CV-Library never gets a job description, asks, a rate line or Rescore role; its CV can still be tailored, saved, downloaded and marked applied.
  • The brief says the fetch and the score take tens of seconds; the page's own note says “a few seconds”, and nothing in the source measures it. The work starts by itself only when the Jobs link is followed over https, and otherwise from a button at the foot of the page.
  • The first score on the job description replaces the role's own score, the one Jobs shows, and can move its tier, into Filtered too, which takes the role off the Jobs list. It is made once per version of the description and never again from this page, even after the prompt or the master CV changes.
  • The page prints CV match as a bare number, “CV match 86.”, where Jobs shows “86%”. The master's tier is a chip beside the heading “The role”, and the copy's is a sentence in its card. When the tier holds, nothing is said and the card score is not drawn.
  • A score made with an older prompt is flagged only on the master's score, which is never made again from this page; the copy's score prints its prompt version and is never flagged.
  • An ask is the scorer's own wording, up to 25 from one advert. Nothing stored ties an ask to a sentence of the advert, flags a must-have or gives a reason, and the stored advert is plain lines with no headings or bullets.
  • No cost is stated before Rescore role is pressed, the button is always enabled, and several presses spend nothing and say so afterwards. The cost note prices the call at about 1.7 US cents, and at most about 6 cents for a reply that reaches its cap; the designs round it up to “about 2p”.
  • Rescore role lists the asks that changed only when the score before it had asks on the same description; after a card score it lists none.
  • Save CV's name is made by the code and cannot be edited: a company the advert does not name is left out, long parts are cut so that the name stays within 150 characters, and each role keeps one saved copy, which the next save replaces.
  • Open the advert on LinkedIn sits among the role's facts, away from Applied. Applied asks first only on this page: the Applied button on a Jobs row marks the role with no question.
  • Start again from master copies the master as it is now, which may be a later version than the copy was made from, and keeps the saved copy and every score.
  • Closed, expired, dismissed and acknowledged roles share one sentence, “not open”, with no date. An applied role's page shows the saved copy as it was sent, has no undo, and is reached only by its address: the Applied list on Jobs does not link to it.
  • Typing is not stored until Rescore role or Save CV is pressed, and nothing labels unsaved edits but the browser's leave-page prompt. After a fetch and score, a Rescore role, a Save CV or a Start again from master, the right-hand side is drawn again whole, so anything the browser alone had drawn there is lost.
  • Waits the brief does not mention, all held in memory and forgotten when the dashboard restarts: 10 minutes for every role after LinkedIn answers 429; 5 minutes for a role after a LinkedIn page that cannot be used; 5 minutes after a scorer's reply that cannot be used; 20 seconds after a scorer error; at least 8 seconds between requests to LinkedIn; up to 150 seconds for a second click on the same role; and 30 minutes for work that was paid for but could not be stored. A wait the page reports is given as a duration, and nothing counts down.
  • Caps the brief does not mention: 4 CV lines cited for an ask, 240 characters of an ask, 200 of a pay quote and 20,000 of a stored description, which the page says when it cuts; a CV of 20,000 characters, 400 lines and 1,000 characters on a line; and one tailored copy per role, shared by every advert in its repost chain.
  • In the editor today a field in focus shows only a change of border colour and ground: the page's focus outline is switched off inside the editor's fields.
  • Both pages need JavaScript for every action; without it they show what is stored.

Questions

Questions that bear on the choice between A, B and C

  1. How should the paper sit in the dark app: white, as the Word file prints (A), a dimmed grey with dark ink (B), or dark with a Paper preview switch (C)? The same choice holds on the CV page.
  2. How should the asks show their evidence at 2560: one row per ask with the cited words left to the sheet (A), a grid that prints the cited words beside the sheet (B), or notes in the sheet's margin (C)?
  3. How small may the sheet be drawn to make room beside it? At 2560 A and C draw it at 100% and B at 80%; at 1280 A draws it at 80%, B at 75% and C at 100%.
  4. At 1280, what should go one press away: the gates, the rate line and the advert (A: Score details and a tab beside Asks), the advert (B: a drawer), or the job description and the scores and steps (C: two drawers)? Should Rescore role and Save CV stay on screen at 1280 whichever is chosen?
  5. Should one action be drawn filled as the page's primary action: Mark applied, as A draws it, Save CV while there are unsaved changes, or none, as B and C draw it?
  6. On a phone, should the CV be a reflowed reading view, as A's CV tab and C draw it, or the A4 sheet scaled to the screen? B names a CV view on the phone and does not draw it.

Questions for one variation, if it is chosen

Three for each variation.

  1. In A, should the selected ask also print the words of its cited lines, under its row?
  2. In A, the gate strip shows the master CV's gates and says the copy passes the same four. Should it switch to the copy's gates once the copy has been rescored?
  3. In A, the selected ask's lines and the asks that cite the line in focus can be lit at once. Should only the selected ask's highlight show while an ask is selected?
  4. In B, at 1280 and on a phone the gates show marks and names only. Should the scorer's gate notes stay at every width, at the cost of fewer asks in view?
  5. In B, on a phone, should Start again from master and Line numbers also be reachable from the Asks view, or is the CV view enough?
  6. In B, the In the advert column is dropped at 1280, where the advert is a drawer. Should it stay there, at the cost of narrower columns?
  7. In C, notes may stand above or below their line, whichever keeps the column nearest its lines; Word only moves comments down, which puts them further away. Which is wanted?
  8. In C, should a stale ask move to “Not evidenced on the CV”, as drawn, or stay beside the reworded line marked “Stale, was met”?
  9. In C, is “Tailor the copy”, a step whose button only moves the focus to the copy, worth keeping?

Questions that stand whichever is chosen

  1. Should a selected ask stay selected while the copy is edited, as all three draw it, or should the highlight follow the pointer and the focus only, as built today?
  2. Jobs says “Full job spec” (the tag) and these pages say “the job description” in sentences. Keep the split, or use one term on both pages?
  3. Should the scorer return, and the product store, the advert sentence behind each ask (B and C draw it) and a reason under each partly or missing ask (C draws it)?
  4. The brief asks for a stable number on every line; the build numbers lines by position and finds a cited line again by its words. Keep positions, or build a stable id for each line?
  5. Undo and Redo do not exist today; A draws them in its toolbar on this page, and A and C on the CV page. Is a history of edits in the editor worth building?
