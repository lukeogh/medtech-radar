Insights, three variations: notes

Caution: Unreviewed. These screens were drawn and self-checked by their authors; the review and fix round was skipped this time, so expect small faults.

A, B and C are labels, not a ranking. Each variation takes one position on the questions the audit left open, and its section says which. Their proposal lists differ in length only because the screens draw different things. Every company, person and product on the screens is invented.

A. Above the bar

Rationale

Insights A draws a line at 40 and lists only what is above it, in two bands, with everything under the line as one count and one link at the foot of the list. The urgent band comes first and is drawn even when it is empty, each row is one event with why it matters, and a reading pane beside the list holds the next move, drafts, sources and the company’s thread and facts. The list opens with when the signal watcher last ran and which sources failed, and it uses the list-and-detail layout of Jobs and most of its keys.

The position it takes

Insights holds company signals only: regulation and general market news are not collected and have no place on the page, and today’s job-market panel leaves it. The bar is 40, the scorer’s own line for “not a signal”, 75 and over is urgent and always first, and “this week” runs from Monday with no memory of a visit.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • A signal at 75 or more is always the first row, with its next move and actions one key away.
  • The list stays short, because everything under 40 (85 on the sample day, 252 on the live day) is one count.
  • Every row is a company you could act on, read with J and K without leaving the page.
  • At 2560 and 1280 the reason, next move, drafts, sources and the company’s thread sit beside the list.

Costs

  • On a quiet week, the usual case, the page is two near-empty bands and a count.
  • Anything the scorer places under 40 is out of sight until the link is followed, so the page relies on 40 being the right bar.
  • Regulation changes that affect software, which the working view asks to read, have no place on the page.
  • It is closer to triage than to the weekly read the working view describes, with no weekly summary and no history of earlier weeks.

Proposals it draws

  • Three bands: “Urgent, 75 and over”, drawn even when empty, “Worth a look, 40 to 74”, and “Below the bar, under 40” as a count with “Show them in the list”.
  • This week first inside the band, “Found this week, since 9 Nov”, then “Found in earlier weeks, still open”, with “Show 4 more from earlier weeks”.
  • A reading pane beside the list: why it matters, next move, drafts and sources, and the company’s thread, facts, contact log, other signals and roles on Jobs.
  • Keys: J and K to move, E for Done, D for Dismiss and O to open the source, with Previous and Next.
  • A “Done and dismissed” fold, Undo last in the page header, and a line under Done and Dismiss saying what each writes and where it is undone.
  • “Phone push armed” or “Phone push is off” in the urgent band’s header.
  • In the stalled notice, the watcher’s schedule and limit (“Every 6 hours, from midnight”, “Late after 7 hours without a run”) and “4 scheduled runs since then left no record”.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: the lists that “Show them in the list” and “Show 4 more from earlier weeks” open, the opened “Done and dismissed” and “Sources” folds, and a signal’s own screen on a phone.

B. The week

Rationale

Insights B lays the page out as a weekly briefing, with each week opening on one line of counts at the bar of 40. Items above the bar are set out in full, why each matters beside the next move and the report it was scored from, while the signals under the bar stay one link away and earlier weeks fold to their counts. Anything at 75 or more that is still open is pinned above the weeks whatever its date, and a side column at 2560 holds the sources and a second list of regulation and market news.

The position it takes

Insights is for weekly reading and includes news, with regulation and market news as a second list that needs new sources and a scale of its own. Urgent items do not wait for Monday, since open signals at 75 or more stay pinned above the weeks, and the weekly bar is 40, so a week’s line agrees with Home’s trend line.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • A typical week, its urgent item and the state of the sources fit one 2560 screen, read top to bottom.
  • Why it matters and the next move are on the page for every item, with no click and no pane to open.
  • A week is a fixed unit whose counts match Home’s trend line, and earlier weeks shrink to one line each.
  • Regulation and market news are read beside company signals, each with why it matters and the next move.

Costs

  • A heavy week of 31 items at 40 or more runs to several screens, and at 1280 the urgent item and about three of the week’s items show before scrolling.
  • Each item is 80 to 200px tall, so scanning many is slower than in a list or a table.
  • Weeks run by the day a signal was found, so on a Monday the current week is nearly empty.
  • The news list needs new sources to keep up and a second scale to trust, and none of that exists today.

Proposals it draws

  • A weekly view: weeks Monday to Sunday by the day found, each opening with its count line (“Week of 9 Nov: 9 above the bar, 23 below”), and earlier weeks folded with lines such as “3 done, 2 still open”.
  • “Urgent, whatever the date”: open signals at 75 or more pinned above the weeks.
  • Each item set out in full, why it matters beside the next move, with Mark done and Dismiss on every item and a line saying what each writes.
  • Band dividers inside the week: “Relevance 70 to 74” and “Relevance 40 to 69”.
  • “Regulation and market news” as a second list on a scale of its own, from four new regulation sources and the trade press (regulation is not collected today, and market news is scored as noise).
  • Sources counted by tier, with when each tier was last read, the failures named, and “Show all 26 sources by name”.
  • “Check now” in the sources column on every day, with what it does beside it.
  • Each item’s provenance on one line, such as “Scored from one report in …”, with a link to the company record.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: an opened earlier week, the signals below 40, the full list of sources, and a closed item’s one line with Undo.

C. Signals table

Rationale

Insights C puts every signal in one sortable table, a row per event, with everything under 40 behind a selected chip: out of the way, one click from view and never deleted. The table opens on relevance, high to low, so a lone urgent item is the first row, tagged and already open with why it matters, the next move, what the figure rests on and its sources. The same rows grouped by company are the watchlist, so the two pages read one set of data.

The position it takes

Insights and the watchlist are one set of data with two views, By signal and By company, and the table is for auditing as much as reading. Insights holds company signals only, with no regulation or general news, the bar is a filter set at 40, and done and dismissed rows sit behind the State chip.

What it gives and what it costs

Gives

  • Every signal on file is reachable in one table, and the bar keeps the 85 open signals below 40 out of the way without deleting them.
  • Sorting and the facets answer audit questions quickly, such as every funding round, everything dismissed or one week’s finds.
  • The next move is on every row, and at 2560 all 17 rows and the open one fit on one screen.
  • Insights and the watchlist cannot disagree, because they are one set of data.

Costs

  • Why it matters needs a row opened, and a table is a less settled weekly read than items set out in full.
  • Sorting by another column moves the urgent row from the top, and when several items are urgent none opens itself.
  • There are more controls to learn, the sort heads, three facets and the bar chip, and on a phone the table keeps one column and changing the sort is a desktop task.
  • One page answering for two navigation entries blurs where the watchlist lives.

Proposals it draws

  • One sortable table: Relevance, Type, Company, Headline, Found, Next move and State.
  • Facet chips for Type, Region and State, and the bar as a chip, “40 and over”, selected, with “Show the 85 open signals below 40”.
  • A view switch, “By signal | By company”, with By company as the watchlist.
  • Rows that open in place: why it matters, what the relevance rests on, each source, the company’s region, window and thread, and whether the phone was told.
  • The Urgent tag, a mark and a word, with “1 urgent” in the counts and a lone urgent row that opens itself.
  • “Open” as the word for the stored state “new”, and E for Done and D for Dismiss.
  • A note above the table on whose words the rows are, and that relevance comes in bands.
  • Every view and filter kept in the address, such as insights?view=signal&bar=40&state=open&sort=relevance.

It also draws everything listed under “What all three draw”. Not drawn on any frame: the By company view, the drafts, and the line saying how a score was made (“Scored when found …, under the rubric of 5 Nov”).

What all three draw

Proposals that A, B and C share, so the lists under A, B and C leave them out. Each is something the product does not do today.

  • The shared app bar of Jobs round 2: Watchlist and CV in the navigation, the command box and the inbox status line.
  • A relevance bar of 40 on the page, with the signals under it one link away (today every stored signal is shown).
  • Urgent signals, 75 and over, counted and set apart (today nothing marks them).
  • One row or item for one event reported by several sources, drawn on the heavy day (today each report is its own card).
  • A signal type, such as Funding round, Spin-off launch, Accelerator entry, First hire or Buying window (there is no type field today).
  • A publication date beside the day found (only the day found is kept today).
  • First-hire signals scored from 40 to 74 (since 5 Nov the rubric scores hiring stories under 40).
  • A link from a signal raised from a job advert to its role on Jobs.
  • When the signal watcher last ran and runs next, and whether the phone push is armed, on Insights itself.
  • Notices when the watcher is late or a source could not be read, each source named with its result, and the age on every figure from a stale run.
  • “Check now” on Insights, with what it does beside it (today it is on the Archive only).
  • “I did it” renamed, Done in A and C and Mark done in B, with an undo (there is none today).
  • Readable source names in place of source ids.
  • An address for each signal and for each filtered view.
  • “Times are UK time”, said once.

Where the working view and the product differ

From the audit’s design facts, as the product stood on 13 Nov 2026.

  • The working view expects news and company insights for the market, regulation changes that affect software included. Insights holds one kind of item, the company signal: general industry news is scored as noise, no regulator or standards body is a source, and the few regulatory items on file scored 5 to 18.
  • You expect to read it weekly, and the page has no weeks: it groups by place (Local, UK, Europe, Elsewhere), then puts the last seven days before everything older, with no “since you last looked”.
  • It shows every signal that is not dismissed, with no bar: 252 of the 265 on file score under 40, the rubric’s own “not a signal”.
  • Nothing marks an urgent signal: a card at 93 looks like a card at 3, the five at 75 or more were 20 to 36 days old on the day, and none has reached the phone.
  • Three sources reporting one event make three cards, and there is no type field, so funding rounds, spin-offs and accelerator entries cannot be told apart, and hiring only by its source id.
  • Since 5 Nov the rubric scores hiring stories under 40, so a first hire can be urgent only as a buying window, which needs the company to be in the contact log already.
  • Region is unreliable for news: 253 of the 265 items sit in “Elsewhere”, plainly European companies among them.
  • The date on a signal is the day Radar found it; the day it was published is not kept.
  • The page does not say when the watcher last ran, and on the audit’s day the watcher had not finished a run for 22.6 hours.
  • At 2560 the page is 24 screens long and the top-scoring item starts 6,862px down, and a job-market panel of rates and gate failures also lives here.

Questions that block the choice

These decide between the variations, so they come first.

  • Should Insights carry regulation and market news as well as company signals (B), or company signals only (A and C)? If news, which regulators and standards bodies should be read, and does news need a bar of its own?
  • How do you mean to read it: as a list worked through beside a reading pane (A), as a weekly briefing (B), or as a table you sort and check (C)?
  • Are Insights and the watchlist one set of data with two views (C), or two pages (A and B)? The answer also settles what the Watchlist entry opens.

Also open, whichever is chosen

  • Whether the bar is 40, which all three use, or 70, the digest’s bar.
  • Where today’s job-market panel of rates and gate failures goes, since none of the three keeps it.
